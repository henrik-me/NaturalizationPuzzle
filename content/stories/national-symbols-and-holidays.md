Symbols and holidays are how a country tells its story without saying a word. The American flag, the national anthem, and the federal holidays each point back to a moment that shaped the country.

## The Capital and a Famous Statue

The **capital of the United States** is **Washington, D.C.** [9]. It is the city where Congress, the President, and the Supreme Court do their work.

The **Statue of Liberty** stands in **New York Harbor** [9]. It was a gift from France in 1886. For millions of immigrants arriving by ship, the statue was the first sight of America.

## The Flag and the Anthem

The U.S. flag has 13 stripes — one for each of the 13 original colonies that broke away from Britain in 1776 [1]. It also has 50 stars, one for every state in the country today [2]. The flag is sometimes called *Old Glory* or *the Stars and Stripes* [1].

The national anthem is *The Star-Spangled Banner* [3]. Francis Scott Key wrote the words in 1814, after watching American forts hold off a British attack on Baltimore. Congress made it the official national anthem in 1931 [3].

## The Motto

The first national motto, *E Pluribus Unum*, is Latin for *out of many, one* [4]. It speaks to a country built from many people, places, and beliefs joined together. The phrase has appeared on U.S. coins since the late 1700s [4].

## Federal Holidays

Federal holidays mark events and people the country chooses to remember. The full list includes Independence Day, Thanksgiving, Memorial Day, Labor Day, Veterans Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Presidents' Day, Columbus Day, Juneteenth, Christmas Day, and New Year's Day [5].

**Independence Day** is on July 4. It marks the day in 1776 when the Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence, and the colonies declared they were free from Britain [6].

**Memorial Day**, the last Monday in May, honors the men and women who died serving in the U.S. military [7].

**Veterans Day**, on November 11, honors all people who have served in the U.S. military [8].
