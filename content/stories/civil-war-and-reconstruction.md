The 1800s were a century of fast change for the United States. The country grew across the continent, fought several wars, and was nearly torn in two by the question of slavery.

## A Growing Country

In 1803, the United States bought a huge area of land from France [1]. This is called the **Louisiana Purchase**. The deal almost doubled the size of the country [1]. Settlers moved west into new states.

The country fought several wars during the 1800s. They include the **War of 1812** with Britain, the **Mexican-American War** in the 1840s, the **Civil War** of the 1860s, and the **Spanish-American War** in 1898 [2]. Any of those is a correct answer to "name one war fought by the United States in the 1800s."

## A Country at War with Itself

In the spring of 1861, the country split in two. Eleven Southern states left the Union, and a four-year war began. It was the deadliest war in U.S. history [3].

The American Civil War was fought between the North and the South from 1861 to 1865 [3]. The North, called the Union, fought to keep the country together. The South, called the Confederacy, fought to leave the Union and protect slavery [3].

Important events of the war include the firing on Fort Sumter, the Battle of Gettysburg, and the Confederate surrender at Appomattox [3].

## Abraham Lincoln

Abraham Lincoln was president during the Civil War [4]. He led the Union, freed enslaved people in the Confederate states, and saved the Union from breaking apart [4]. Lincoln also gave the famous Gettysburg Address [4].

On January 1, 1863, Lincoln signed the Emancipation Proclamation [5]. The order freed enslaved people in the states that were fighting against the Union [5]. It changed the war's purpose: it was now also a war to end slavery.

## Reconstruction and the Amendments

The Civil War ended slavery [6]. After the war, Congress passed three amendments — sometimes called the Reconstruction Amendments — to make the change permanent.

- The **13th Amendment** ended slavery in every state [7].
- The **14th Amendment** says that all persons born or naturalized in the United States are U.S. citizens, and it promises equal protection of the laws [7].
- The **15th Amendment** gave Black men the right to vote in 1870 [8].

## Voting Rights After the War

By law, all men got the right to vote in 1870 with the 15th Amendment [8]. In practice, many Black Americans were blocked from voting in the South for almost a century after, until the Voting Rights Act of 1965 [8].

Women still could not vote. The women's rights movement was led by Susan B. Anthony, Elizabeth Cady Stanton, and Lucretia Mott, among others [9]. Their work helped lead to the 19th Amendment in 1920, which gave women the right to vote [9].
