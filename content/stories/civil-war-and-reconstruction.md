In the spring of 1861, the country split in two. Eleven Southern states left the Union, and a four-year war began. It was the deadliest war in U.S. history.

## A Country at War

The American Civil War was fought between the North and the South from 1861 to 1865 [1]. The North, called the Union, fought to keep the country together. The South, called the Confederacy, fought to leave the Union and protect slavery [1].

Important events of the war include the firing on Fort Sumter, the Battle of Gettysburg, and the Confederate surrender at Appomattox [1].

## Abraham Lincoln

Abraham Lincoln was president during the Civil War [2]. He led the Union, freed enslaved people in the Confederate states, and saved the Union from breaking apart [2]. Lincoln also gave the famous Gettysburg Address [2].

On January 1, 1863, Lincoln signed the Emancipation Proclamation [3]. The order freed enslaved people in the states that were fighting against the Union [3]. It changed the war's purpose: it was now also a war to end slavery.

## Reconstruction and the Amendments

The Civil War ended slavery [4]. After the war, Congress passed three amendments — sometimes called the Reconstruction Amendments — to make the change permanent.

- The **13th Amendment** ended slavery in every state [5].
- The **14th Amendment** says that all persons born or naturalized in the United States are U.S. citizens, and it promises equal protection of the laws [5].
- The **15th Amendment** gave Black men the right to vote in 1870 [6].

## Voting Rights After the War

By law, all men got the right to vote in 1870 with the 15th Amendment [6]. In practice, many Black Americans were blocked from voting in the South for almost a century after, until the Voting Rights Act of 1965 [6].

Women still could not vote. The women's rights movement was led by Susan B. Anthony, Elizabeth Cady Stanton, and Lucretia Mott, among others [7]. Their work helped lead to the 19th Amendment in 1920, which gave women the right to vote [7].
