Long before the United States existed, people from many places came to the land that became America. They were drawn by hopes for land, jobs, freedom, and new lives.

## Why People Came

Many of the colonists came to North America for **freedom** [1]. Some wanted **religious freedom**. Others wanted **political freedom** or **economic freedom**. Others came to **escape persecution** in their home countries. Some came simply for the chance to **own land** [1].

## People Already Here

The land was not empty. Many groups of **American Indians** had lived here for thousands of years before Europeans arrived [2]. Tribes like the Cherokee, the Navajo, the Sioux, the Apache, the Iroquois, the Pueblo, and many others had their own languages, governments, and ways of life [2].

The first colonists also brought a system that would shape American history for centuries: **slavery**. Africans were brought to the colonies and forced to work, mostly on farms in the South [2].

## Toward Independence

By the 1760s, the **13 colonies** were tired of British rule. The colonists were upset about **high taxes** that they did not vote on. They were upset that British soldiers were living in their houses. They were upset that they had **no say** in the British Parliament [3].

In 1775, fighting broke out between colonial militias and British soldiers in Massachusetts. The American Revolution had begun [3].

## The Declaration of Independence

In **1776**, the Second Continental Congress wrote the **Declaration of Independence** [4]. It was mostly written by **Thomas Jefferson**. The Declaration was adopted on **July 4, 1776**. We celebrate that day as Independence Day each year [4].

The Declaration named **King George III** of Britain as the king who had wronged the colonies. It then said the colonies were now free states [4].

## A New Government

After the war ended, the new United States needed a stable government. Leaders met in **Philadelphia** in **1787** at the **Constitutional Convention** [5]. The Constitution was written there. **George Washington** led the meeting. **Benjamin Franklin** also took part [5].

When the Constitution was sent to the states for approval, **The Federalist Papers** were written by **James Madison, Alexander Hamilton, and John Jay** [5]. These essays argued for the Constitution and helped it win support.

The Constitution was approved. **George Washington** became the first **President of the United States** in 1789 [5]. He is often called the **"Father of Our Country."**
