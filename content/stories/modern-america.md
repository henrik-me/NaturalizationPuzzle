The end of the Cold War in 1991 opened a new chapter for the United States. Old conflicts ended. New ones started. American culture and American technology reached almost every corner of the world.

## The Persian Gulf War

The **Persian Gulf War** was a short conflict in 1990–1991. The United States entered the war to **force the Iraqi army out of Kuwait** [1]. Iraq had invaded its small neighbor in August 1990. A coalition of more than 30 countries, led by the United States, drove Iraqi forces back across the border in early 1991.

## September 11, 2001

On **September 11, 2001**, **terrorists attacked the United States** [2]. Hijackers crashed two airplanes into the World Trade Center towers in New York City and a third into the Pentagon near Washington, D.C. A fourth plane crashed in Pennsylvania after passengers fought back. Nearly 3,000 people died. It was the deadliest attack on U.S. soil in modern history.

## After September 11

After the attacks, the United States entered new conflicts. The **War in Afghanistan** (2001–2021) was launched to remove the Taliban government and disrupt al-Qaeda. The **War in Iraq** (2003–2011) overthrew Saddam Hussein's government. The U.S. also sent troops in **Operation Inherent Resolve** in the 2010s to fight ISIS in Iraq and Syria [3].

## American Indian Tribes Today

Hundreds of **American Indian tribes** are still part of the United States [4]. Among them are the **Cherokee, Navajo, Sioux, Apache, Iroquois, Pueblo, Lakota, Seminole, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Mohegan, Crow, Inuit**, and many more [4]. Each tribe has its own government, language, and traditions.

## American Innovation

The United States has long been a leader in **innovation** — new ideas, inventions, and ways of doing things [5]. Some examples include the **light bulb** (Thomas Edison), the **airplane** (the Wright brothers), the **assembly line** (Henry Ford), the **moon landing** (NASA, 1969), the **personal computer**, **integrated circuits**, the **Internet**, and modern smartphones [5].
