After World War II, the United States faced a new kind of struggle. There was no shooting war between the two big powers. But the rivalry shaped world politics for nearly 50 years.

## The Two Superpowers

After 1945, two countries stood above all others: the United States and the **Soviet Union**. The Soviet Union was the United States' **main rival during the Cold War** [1]. The two sides backed different forms of government and economy. The U.S. backed democracy and capitalism. The Soviet Union backed **communism** [1].

A main concern for the United States during the Cold War was the **spread of communism** to other countries [1]. The U.S. and its allies tried to stop communism from spreading.

## Eisenhower

**Dwight D. Eisenhower** was a key American leader of this era [2]. He was the **general** who led the Allied forces in Europe during World War II. After the war, he was elected the **34th president** in 1952 and served until 1961. Eisenhower also signed the law that built the **Interstate Highway System**, the network of high-speed roads that link the country today [2].

## The Korean War

The **Korean War** started in 1950 and ended in 1953. The United States entered the war to **stop the spread of communism** [3]. North Korea, backed by the Soviet Union and China, had invaded South Korea. The U.S. fought on the side of South Korea. The war ended without a clear victory. Korea is still divided today.

## The Vietnam War

The **Vietnam War** ran from the mid-1950s through 1975. The United States entered to **stop the spread of communism** in Southeast Asia [4]. North Vietnam, with support from the Soviet Union and China, was trying to take over South Vietnam. The U.S. fought on the side of South Vietnam. The war ended with North Vietnam's victory in 1975.
