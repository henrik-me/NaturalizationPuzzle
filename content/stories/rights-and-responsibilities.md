American citizens have rights that the government cannot take away. Citizens also have duties they should carry out. Together these make a working democracy.

## Rights for Everyone in the United States

Some rights belong to **everyone living in the United States**, not just to citizens [1]. The Bill of Rights names these rights. They include:

- Freedom of speech.
- Freedom of religion.
- Freedom of the press (the news media).
- Freedom of assembly (gathering in public).
- The right to ask the government to fix problems.

These rights are protected even for people who are not yet citizens [1].

## Voting Rights — Many Amendments

Voting is one of the great rights of citizens. The Constitution protects the right to vote in many ways. **Four amendments** changed who can vote in federal elections [2]:

- The **15th Amendment** says a citizen cannot be denied the vote because of race or color.
- The **19th Amendment** gives women the right to vote.
- The **24th Amendment** ends the poll tax (a fee for voting).
- The **26th Amendment** sets the voting age at **18**.

To vote in a federal election, a person must be a **U.S. citizen who is at least 18 years old** [2].

## Rights Only for Citizens

Some rights belong **only to U.S. citizens** [3]. These include:

- The right to vote in a federal election.
- The right to run for federal office.
- The right to serve on a jury (in most courts; rules vary).
- The right to a U.S. passport.

## Responsibilities

Citizens also have duties. **Two responsibilities of citizens only** are [3]:

- **Serve on a jury** when called.
- **Vote in a federal election**.

Other duties belong to **everyone** living here, citizen or not [3]:

- **Obey the law**.
- **Pay federal income tax** by **April 15** each year [4].
- **Serve on a jury** if called (where required).
- **Register with the Selective Service** for men ages 18–25.

## The Pledge and the Oath

The **Pledge of Allegiance** is a promise of loyalty to the U.S. flag and to the country [3]. New citizens take a longer promise called the **Oath of Allegiance**. They give up loyalty to other countries, swear to defend the Constitution, and promise to serve the country if needed [3].
