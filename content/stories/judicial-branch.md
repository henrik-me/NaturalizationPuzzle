The judicial branch is the system of courts. The courts decide what the laws mean and whether the laws are fair under the Constitution.

## What the Courts Do

The judicial branch reviews laws. It explains laws. It resolves disputes between people. And it decides if a law goes against the Constitution [1]. This power to strike down laws that conflict with the Constitution is called **judicial review** [2].

## The Supreme Court

The **Supreme Court** is the **highest court** in the United States [3]. It is one part of the judicial branch. Below it sit the federal courts of appeals, the federal district courts, and other special federal courts [3].

The Supreme Court has **9 seats** today: one Chief Justice and eight associate justices [3]. Most of the time, **5 justices** are needed to decide a case. That is the simple majority of nine [3].

## Why Justices Serve for Life

Supreme Court justices serve **for life** — that is, until they choose to retire, die, or are removed. The Constitution says they may serve "during good Behaviour" [3]. The framers picked life terms for an important reason. They wanted justices to make fair calls based on the Constitution, not on what was popular at the moment [3]. A judge who never has to face an election can rule against the President or against Congress without fear.

## Today's Chief Justice

The **Chief Justice of the United States** today is **John Roberts**. He has served in that role since 2005 [4]. The Chief Justice leads the Supreme Court and presides over impeachment trials of the President in the Senate.
