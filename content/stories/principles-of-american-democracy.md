The United States is a country of written rules. The most important rule book is the Constitution. Every other law in the country must agree with it.

## A Government of the People

The form of government of the United States is a **constitutional federal republic** — also called a **representative democracy** [1]. The people elect leaders. Those leaders make the laws. Power is shared between the federal government and the states [1].

The **U.S. Constitution** is the **supreme law of the land** [2]. It does three big jobs [2]:

- It sets up the government.
- It says what the government can and cannot do.
- It protects the basic rights of the people.

The Constitution starts with the words **"We the People."** Those three words mean one big thing. The power of the government comes from the people. It does not come from a king [2].

## Changing the Constitution

The Constitution can be changed. A change is called an **amendment**. The change is hard on purpose. Two-thirds of Congress must propose it. Then three-fourths of the states must agree to it [3].

The Constitution has **27 amendments** today [3]. The first ten are called the **Bill of Rights**. They protect basic rights. These include free speech, freedom of religion, a free press, and the right to a fair trial [4].

## Founding Ideas

The **Declaration of Independence** was adopted on July 4, 1776. It is important for two reasons. It said the colonies were free from Britain. It also said why the colonies had the right to be free [5]. The famous words **"Life, Liberty, and the pursuit of Happiness"** come from the Declaration [5].

Two big ideas show up in both the Declaration and the Constitution. One is that **all people are equal**. The other is that **the government gets its power from the people** [5][2].

The framers did not start from nothing. They learned from older documents. The most important were the Magna Carta, the English Bill of Rights, and the Declaration of Independence [6].

## Rule of Law and Economy

The **rule of law** is a simple idea. Everyone must follow the law. Leaders must follow the law. Government workers must follow the law. No one is above the law [7].

The economic system of the United States is a **capitalist** or **market economy**. People and private firms own most of the property. They decide what to make and what to sell [8].
