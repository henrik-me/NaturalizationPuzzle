The United States has two layers of government. The federal government in Washington handles the big national jobs. The state governments handle most of the day-to-day work that shapes daily life.

## Powers of the Federal Government

Some powers belong only to the federal government [1]. These include:

- **Print money**.
- **Declare war**.
- **Make treaties** with other countries.
- **Maintain a national military** (the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, and so on).

Only the federal government can do these jobs. The states cannot do them on their own [1].

## Powers of the State Governments

Other powers belong only to the states [1]. These include:

- **Provide schooling and education** in the state.
- **Provide protection** through state and local police.
- **Provide safety** through fire departments.
- **Give a driver's license**.
- **Approve zoning and land use** for cities and towns.

The states do most of the work that touches a typical person's day [1].

## The 10th Amendment

So how do we know which power belongs to which level? The answer is in the Constitution. The **10th Amendment** sets the rule. Any power **not given to the federal government** by the Constitution is left to the **states** or to the people [2].

This is why the United States is called a **federal** system. Power is split. The federal government holds the powers the Constitution gives it. Everything else stays with the states [2].

## Your State Government

Each state has its own government. Each state is run by a **governor** elected by the people of that state. Each state also has its own **capital city** — the city where the state government meets [3].
