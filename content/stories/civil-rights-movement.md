The fight for equal rights in America stretches across the whole story of the country. Each generation has pushed it forward.

## The Long Road

After the Civil War, the **15th Amendment** said that the right to vote could not be denied because of race or color [1]. In effect, this meant **all men got the right to vote in 1870** [1]. But for nearly a century after, many Black Americans were blocked from voting in the South. Real voting rights for all came only with the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

Women were left out for even longer. **Susan B. Anthony**, **Elizabeth Cady Stanton**, **Lucretia Mott**, and **Sojourner Truth** were leaders of the women's rights movement in the 1800s [2]. Their work helped lead to the **19th Amendment** in **1920**, which gave **all women the right to vote** [3].

## The Civil Rights Movement

In the 1950s and 1960s, the **civil rights movement** worked to **end racial discrimination** in the United States [4]. It used marches, court cases, voter drives, and acts of nonviolent protest. The movement led to the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

## Martin Luther King, Jr.

**Martin Luther King, Jr.** is famous for many things [5]:

- He **fought for the civil rights** of African Americans.
- He led the movement using **nonviolence**.
- He **worked for equality** for all Americans.
- He gave the famous **"I Have a Dream"** speech in 1963 in Washington, D.C.
- He won the **Nobel Peace Prize** in 1964.

Dr. King was killed in 1968. His birthday is now a federal holiday in his honor [5].
